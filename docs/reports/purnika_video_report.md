## 3. Slide rendering

Every slide is drawn pixel-by-pixel with PIL — no PowerPoint export, no HTML
screenshotting. Each of the five parts has its own theme (background, accent,
text and subtext), and each slide carries an accent bar, a rounded part badge, a
wrapped title, up to three wrapped bullets, and a right-hand visual panel.

Five visual panel types are drawn programmatically:

| `visual_type` | Rendering |
| --- | --- |
| `formula` | LaTeX via matplotlib mathtext → transparent RGBA → scaled to fit the panel |
| `diagram` | Concentric cell/atom figure with organelle dots and a caption |
| `process` | Three-step flowchart with arrowheads |
| `comparison` | Side-by-side labelled comparison blocks |
| `alert` | Hazard triangle with "CRITICAL NEET TRAP" caption |

**Per-scene colour override.** The segmentation prompt (Section 4.4) emits a
`background_color` hex per part. `resolve_theme()` honours it while keeping the
part's accent and text colours, so a director can retint a scene without a code
change and contrast rules still hold.

Rendering cost: **0.10 s mean per slide** (5 themes in 0.50 s). The `EXAMPLE`
theme is the outlier at ~0.28 s because it invokes matplotlib for the formula.

Evidence: `backend/outputs/slides/theme_*.png`, regenerated by
`python backend/scripts/benchmark_video.py`.

## 4. Animation engine

The scene JSON carries `animation_type` of `fade_in | slide_left | zoom`.
`apply_scene_animation()` dispatches it; unknown values fall back to `fade_in`.

| `animation_type` | Transform |
| --- | --- |
| `fade_in` | Ken Burns drift, scale 1.00 → 1.03 |
| `zoom` | Push-in, scale 1.00 → 1.06 |
| `slide_left` | Enters from the right edge, ease-out cubic over 0.6 s |

`resolve_scene_duration()` reconciles narration length with the segmenter's
`duration_hint_seconds`: the audio is authoritative, a scene is never shorter
than its narration, and a longer hint may hold the slide for up to 3 extra
seconds. Without this cap a bad hint could stretch a scene arbitrarily.

### Defect found and fixed: zoom was clipping the slide

Zoom animations centre-crop the frame — a scale of *z* removes
`W · (1 − 1/z) / 2` pixels from each side. At the original 1.12 zoom that is
**69 px horizontally**, while the badge and title were drawn at x = 40. Frame
inspection at t = 45 s showed the badge cut in half, the title reading "olving
NCERT Examples", and the formula bleeding off the right edge.

Three coordinated fixes:

1. A **title-safe area** (`SAFE_MARGIN_X = 80`, `SAFE_MARGIN_Y = 50`), with all
   text and the accent bar moved inside it — standard broadcast practice.
2. `MAX_ZOOM` reduced to **1.06**, which crops ~36 px horizontally and ~20 px
   vertically, comfortably inside the safe margin. The constant is documented
   as being bound to the safe area, so the two cannot drift apart silently.
3. Formula images are **scaled to fit** the visual panel. Matplotlib sizes its
   output from the expression, so a long formula previously overflowed the
   slide regardless of zoom.

Verified by extracting a frame at peak zoom: badge, title, all three bullets,
the complete formula and the subtitle band are all fully visible.

## 5. Karaoke subtitles

Word timings come from Edge-TTS `WordBoundary` events (Pallika's module), so the
highlight follows the actual speech rather than an estimate. Words are wrapped
into lines by measured pixel width, the active word is highlighted in yellow,
and a semi-transparent backing bar keeps text readable over any theme.

Three properties make it cheap at render time:

* **Layout is computed once per scene** — wrapping, alpha mask and band geometry
  are time-invariant.
* **Frames are cached by highlighted-word index** — a scene has at most
  *N + 1* distinct subtitle images, not one per video frame.
* **The active word is found by binary search** over word start times rather
  than rescanning every boundary on every frame.

Timestamp integrity depends on Pallika's `shift_word_boundaries()`: Edge-TTS
timestamps are relative to untrimmed audio, so trimming lead-in silence without
correcting them makes every highlight fire early.

## 6. Rendering performance — the optimisation study

The Section 10.2 target is **< 90 s for a full video**. The first measured run
was **585 s**, with 94% of wall time inside `write_videofile`.

### Step 1 — the encoder was not the bottleneck

Switching x264 from `medium` to `veryfast` changed total time from 585 s to
625 s, i.e. nothing beyond run-to-run noise. That ruled out the encoder and
pointed at Python-side frame generation.

### Step 2 — isolating the cost

`scripts/profile_frame_pipeline.py` encodes the same 10-second slide with each
feature switched on independently (240 frames, 1280×720):

| Variant | Time | Throughput | Overhead vs baseline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| static (encoder floor) | 1.77 s | 136.0 fps | — |
| ken_burns | 3.06 s | 78.4 fps | +73% |
| **subs_composite** (original) | **21.80 s** | **11.0 fps** | **+1132%** |
| subs_burn (optimised) | 3.56 s | 67.5 fps | +101% |
| kenburns + composite (original pipeline) | 20.92 s | 11.5 fps | +1082% |
| **kenburns + burn (current pipeline)** | **4.79 s** | **50.2 fps** | **+171%** |

Subtitle compositing, not the zoom and not the encoder, was consuming over 90%
of frame-generation time.

### Step 3 — the root cause

`cProfile` over 120 composited frames put 8.9 s of 11.4 s inside MoviePy's
`video/tools/drawing.py::blit`, at ~37 ms per call, plus 1.5 s in `np.dstack`.
MoviePy 1.0.3's generic blit copies the entire destination frame, expands the
alpha mask across three channels with `dstack` on every frame, and performs the
blend in float64 — all for an overlay occupying the bottom eighth of the screen.

### Step 4 — the fix

`SubtitleEngine.burn_onto()` replaces `CompositeVideoClip` for the subtitle
layer and does the blend itself:

* alpha stored once as `(band_h, W, 1)` float32 and **broadcast** — no per-frame
  `dstack`;
* subtitle bands cached **pre-multiplied by alpha**, so each frame costs one
  multiply and one add;
* only the subtitle band is touched, not the full 1280×720 frame;
* the base frame is centre-cropped in the same pass, which also fixed the zoom
  anchoring (MoviePy was anchoring the enlarged frame at the top-left instead of
  pushing into the centre).

An intermediate change — rendering the overlay as a bottom band rather than a
full-screen layer — contributed a further ~37% before the custom compositor.

### Result

| Stage | Before | After | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Encode (incl. frame generation) | 593.39 s | 111.63 s | **5.3× faster** |
| TTS | 28.06 s | 11.61 s | (network variance) |
| Slide rendering | 0.25 s | 0.29 s | unchanged |
| Animation setup | 0.27 s | 0.26 s | unchanged |
| Subtitle setup | 0.17 s | 0.15 s | unchanged |
| **Total** | **625.18 s** | **125.37 s** | **5.0× faster** |

Real-time factor improved from 8.48× to **1.70×** — the pipeline now renders a
73.7 s video in 125 s.

### Honest status against the target

**The < 90 s target is not yet met for a full 5-scene, 74-second video.**
125 s is 1.4× over. Three observations:

1. Encoding is now **89%** of remaining time and scales with video length. A
   shorter video meets the target today: at 1.70× real time, anything under
   ~53 s of output finishes inside 90 s.
2. Scene rendering is **embarrassingly parallel** — five scenes are encoded
   sequentially but share nothing. Rendering them in parallel processes and
   concatenating at the container level (stream copy, no re-encode) is the
   obvious next step and should approach the 90 s target directly.
3. Cached generations return instantly via the MongoDB cache layer, so the cold
   figure is the worst case, not the typical one.

## 7. Portability fix: fonts

Both renderers hardcoded `C:\Windows\Fonts\arial.ttf`. On any non-Windows host
that path does not exist and PIL silently falls back to its bitmap default font,
which ignores the requested size — slides would still render, but with
unreadable text, and no error would be raised.

`app/video/fonts.py` resolves the first available TrueType face from a
per-platform candidate list (Windows Arial/Segoe, Linux DejaVu/Liberation, macOS
Arial/Helvetica), caches by size and weight, and exposes `font_is_scalable()` so
the benchmark records which face was actually used. Titles and badges now render
in a real bold face rather than a synthetic one.

## 8. VMake.ai integration — status

`vmake_integration.py` is complete: per-scene-type and per-subject prompts for
all 15 combinations, the `image-to-video` API call, and compositing of a
transparent slide over a darkened, looped background clip.

**It has never executed.** The assembler previously read the key via
`getattr(self.settings, 'vmake_api_key', '')` against a Settings object that had
no such field, so `is_enabled()` was permanently `False` and the entire branch
was unreachable dead code.

Fixed: `vmake_api_key` and `vmake_enabled` are now real settings, and
`Settings.vmake_active` requires **both** a key and an explicit flag. The
assembler falls back to a standard animated slide if generation or download
fails, so an expired key degrades gracefully rather than failing the job.

> submission checklist item "VMake.ai integration for background clips" cannot
> honestly be ticked, and the endpoint/response shape in the guide is unverified
> against the live API.

## 9. Integration status

The assembler is wired into `JobQueue.run_generation`, which drives the
WebSocket progress stages and uploads the finished MP4 to Cloudinary.

Scenes currently come from `scene_presets.get_fallback_scenes()`, not from RAG
retrieval plus LLM segmentation. Those presets deliberately exercise every part
theme, every visual type and every animation type, so they double as the test
and benchmark fixture. Swapping in `scene_segmenter.py` output requires no
change to this module — the contract is the scene dict.

## 10. Known limitations

1. **< 90 s target not met** for a full-length video (125 s). Parallel scene
   rendering is the identified fix.
2. **VMake.ai never executed** — no API key.
3. **Diagram panels are generic.** `visual_type: diagram` draws a stylised
   cell/atom, not the actual NCERT figure referenced by `diagram_refs`.
   Extracting real figures during PDF ingestion would replace this.
4. **`pyloudnorm` absent**, so audio loudness (Pallika's module) is measured as
   RMS dBFS rather than true LUFS.
5. **MoviePy pinned to 1.0.3.** MoviePy 2.x removes `moviepy.editor` and renames
   the clip API; the pin is recorded in `requirements.txt`.
